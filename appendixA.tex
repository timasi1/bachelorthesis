To further simplify the hopping Hamiltonian $H_\text{hop.}$, we begin by showing 
\begin{align}
    (\mathbf{d}_i \cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}})² &= (d_x \hat{\sigma}_x + d_y \hat{\sigma}_y + d_z \hat{\sigma}_z)² \\
    &= (d_x² + d_y² + d_z²)\hat{I}_3 + d_xd_y\{\hat{\sigma}_x , \hat{\sigma}_y\} + d_yd_z\{\hat{\sigma}_y , \hat{\sigma}_z\} + d_xd_z\{\hat{\sigma}_x , \hat{\sigma}_z\} = \hat{I}_2,
\end{align}
where $\hat{I}_2$ denotes the unity matrix in two dimensions, following from basic operations with the Pauli-matrices, where $\{\hat{A} , \hat{B}\} = \hat{A}\hat{B}+\hat{B}\hat{A}$ and $|d_i| = 1$.
Next, we can use Euler's formula 
\begin{align}
    e^{i\Phi(\mathbf{d_i}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}})} = \sum_k \frac{(i\Phi)^{2k}}{(2k)!}\hat{I}_3 + \sum_k  \frac{(i\Phi)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!} (\mathbf{d_i}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}) = \cos(\Phi)\hat{I}_3 + i \sin(\Phi) (\mathbf{d_i}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}})
    \label{eq: hamilton exp}
\end{align}
or analogously for the primed parameters as $\Phi'$.
Thus, with Eq. \eqref{eq: hamilton exp}, we gather for the hopping Hamiltonian consisting of the Rashba term as in equation \eqref{eq:Rashba_Hamiltonian_soc} and the pure hopping term as in Eq. \eqref{eq: 1_hamiltonian_hopping}
\begin{align}
    H_{\text{hop.}}
&=-
\sum_{\mathbf R}
\Bigg\{
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
c_{\mathbf R,\beta_i}^{\dagger}
\left[t'I_3 - i\lambda'(\mathbf d_i\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\sigma})\right]
c_{\mathbf R,\alpha_i}
\nonumber\\
& \qquad\qquad+
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
c_{\mathbf R-\mathbf a_i,\beta_i}^{\dagger}
\left[t I_3 - i\lambda(\mathbf d_i\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\sigma})\right]
c_{\mathbf R,\alpha_i}
\Bigg\}
+\mathrm{H.c.} \nonumber\\
&= -
\sum_{\mathbf R}
\Bigg\{
\sqrt{t'^2+\lambda'^2}
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
c_{\mathbf R,\beta_i}^\dagger
e^{i\Phi'(\mathbf d_i\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}
c_{\mathbf R,\alpha_i} \nonumber\\
& \qquad\qquad+
\sqrt{t^2+\lambda^2}
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
c_{\mathbf R-\mathbf a_i,\beta_i}^\dagger
e^{i\Phi(\mathbf d_i\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}
c_{\mathbf R,\alpha_i}
\Bigg\}
+\mathrm{H.c.}.
\label{eq: appendix hamiltonian}
\end{align}
Here, we define $\cos(\Phi) = t/\sqrt{t^2 + \lambda^2}$ and $\sin(\Phi) = -\lambda/\sqrt{t^2 + \lambda^2}$ and respecively for the primed parameters $\cos(\Phi') = t'^2/\sqrt{t'^2 + \lambda'^2}$ and $\sin(\Phi') = -\lambda'/\sqrt{t'^2 + \lambda'^2}$.
