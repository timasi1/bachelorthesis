First, we check the sign convention chosen in Eq.~\ref{eq: bloch-Hamiltonian} by using a homogeneous magnetic texture, as depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig: magnetic texture homogenous}.

Finally, Figs.~\ref{fig:homogenous-notexture} compare the band structures (a),(b) without a magnetic texture with thos for the (c),(d) homogeneous texture. 
For $t'= \lambda = \lambda'=0$, the nonmagnetic kagome lattice has two flat bands, with one of them being orbital degenerate. The flatness stems from the fact that the electrons cannot propagate in the lattice because of a missing hopping on the upward-facing triangles. One might expect three flat bands, however it can easily be verified by solving the Hamiltonian
\begin{align}
    \frak{H}(\mathbf{k}) = t\begin{pmatrix}
        0&   e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{a}_1} &   e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{a}_3} \\
          e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{a}_1} & 0 &    e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{a}_2} \\
        e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{a}_3} &   e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{a}_2} & 0 
    \end{pmatrix} + \mathcal{H}_\text{M},
\end{align}
following from $\hat{t}_i = t \hat{I}_2$ and $\hat{t}' = t'\hat{I}_2 = 0$ and removing the spin dependence due to a spin independent Hamiltonian. Solving the matrix with the earlier defined cell-connection vectors $\mathbf{a}_i$, which are triangular ordered, the eigenvalues $E_+ = 2t$ and the twofold degenerate $E_- = -t$ are obtained. Therefore, the upper band is twofold degenerate and the lower one fourfold degenerate, including an orbital degeneracy. 
Now, by adding SOC with $\lambda'=t'=0$, we obtain three flat bands, occurring due to a broken orbital degeneracy. Note that the bands are still Kramers-degenerate.

By adding a magnetic texture, the Kramers degeneracy is broken and therefore there are twice as many bands, due to the magnetic texture breaking time-reversal and the lack of an antiunitary symmetry protecting the degeneracy.

Furthermore, by adding hopping on upward-facing triangles, so $t'\neq0$ or $\lambda'\neq 0$, dispersive bands are obtained, as seen in Fig.~\ref{fig:homogenous-notexture} (b), (d). For no SOC and no magnetic texture, Kramers pairs are expected, resulting in three bands, as observed.  These bands are split by adding SOC or a magnetic texture, therefore resulting in six dispersive bands.
It is interesting to note that there still exists a flat band, or one degenerate flat band for the magnetic lattice. This occurs through the lattice and magnetic geometry, leading to destructive interference. We will not address this further, due to no additional gained knowledge. 
All different cases are not analyzed in detail because we expect for all of them up to six dispersive bands, where degeneracies may occur due to the previously claimed reason.
