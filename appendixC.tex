
In this Chapter we want to compare kagome lattices with no magnetic texture $m_0=0$ and a homogenous one, as depicted in Fig. \ref{fig: magnetic texture homogenous}, to get an idea where the bands stem from and why the used model can be approved. 
Firstly, we will begin to check the sign convention chosen in Eq. \ref{eq: bloch-Hamiltonian} by using a homogenous magnetic texture, as depicted in \ref{fig: magnetic texture homogenous}.

Lastly, the band structures of no magnetic texture $m_0=0$ in Fig. \ref{fig:homogenous-notexture} (a), (b) with the homogenous one, as earlier defined, depicted in Fig. \ref{fig:homogenous-notexture} (c), (d) are compared.
For $t'= \lambda = \lambda'=0$, the nonmagnetic Kagome lattice has two flat bands. The flatness stems from the fact, that the electrons cannot propagate in the lattice because of a missing hopping on the upward-facing triangle. One might expect three flat bands, however it can easily be approved by solving the Hamiltonian
\begin{align}
    \frak{H}(\mathbf{k}) = \begin{pmatrix}
        0&  t e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{{a}_1}} &  t e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{{a}_3}} \\
         t e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{{a}_1}} & 0 &   t e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{{a}_2}} \\
       t e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{{a}_3}} &  t e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{{a}_2}} & 0 
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
following from $\hat{t}_i = t_i \hat{I}_2 = \hat{t}_i$, $\hat{t}' = t'\hat{I}_2 = 0$ and switching the basis to $\left(c_{\mathbf{k}A}^\dagger, c_{\mathbf{k}B}^\dagger, c_{\mathbf{k}C}^\dagger\right)$, dropping out the spin-dependancy because of Kramer's degeneracy. Solving the matrix with the earlier defined Bravais-lattice vectors $\mathbf{a}_i$, the eigenvalues $E_+ = 2t$ and the twofold degenerate $E_- = -t$, which is an orbital degeneracy, are obtained. Therefore, the upper band is twofold degenerate and the lower one fourfold degenerate. 
Now, by adding SOC with $\lambda'=t'=0$, we gather three flat bands, occuring because the orbital degeneracy is broken. Note, that the bands are still Kramer's degenerate.

By adding a magnetic texture the Kramer's degeneracy is broken and therefore there are twice as many bands.

By now adding hopping on upward-facing triangles, so $t'\neq0$ or $\lambda'\neq 0$, dispersive bands are obtained, as seen in \ref{fig:homogenous-notexture} (b), (d). For no SOC and no magnetic texture, Kramer pairs are expected, leading to up to three bands as observed.  It is interesting to note, that there still exists a flat band, or two for the magnetic lattice, occuring through destructive interference by the geometrical structure of the kagome lattice. All different cases are not analysed in detail because, we expect for all of them six dispersive bands.
